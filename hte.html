CASE STUDY 01 · HUMAN-CENTERED DESIGN

Designing for curiosity in the classroom.

A human-centered design project focused on making learning about energy more engaging for fourth-grade students at High Tech Elementary.

ROLEResearcher · Documentarian · Communications Liaison
METHODSInterviews · Personas · Empathy · Testing
OUTPUTHands-on learning experience
SETTINGHigh Tech Elementary
IN-PERSON TESTING · HIGH TECH ELEMENTARY
01 / THE CHALLENGE

Start with the learner, not the lesson plan.

The project asked the team to solve a nonprofit challenge through human-centered design. The goal was to create a learning experience around energy that could hold the attention of fourth-grade students while remaining feasible for the classroom.

Instead of jumping directly into a solution, we first looked at the people, environment, and constraints shaping the experience.

How might we make learning about energy more engaging for fourth graders?
02 / RESEARCH

Listen. Observe. Synthesize.

Research combined primary and secondary sources with direct input from the teacher and student users. Personas and empathy work helped turn observations into a clearer picture of user needs and the classroom context.

03 / DEFINE + IDEATE

Turn research into a design opportunity.

The team translated the research into problem statements, design criteria, and multiple concepts. The work was evaluated through the lenses of usability, feasibility, desirability, and affordability.

IDEATION / CONCEPT DEVELOPMENT
04 / PROTOTYPE + TEST

Put the idea in front of real users.

The final concept was organized into rotating hands-on stations. Students experienced the activities in person, giving the team an opportunity to observe the experience and gather direct feedback rather than relying only on assumptions.

USER TESTING / PARTICIPATION
USER TESTING / CLASSROOM CONTEXT
05 / ITERATION

Evidence shaped the next version.

Testing and evaluation fed back into the concept. The project documented what worked, what needed adjustment, and how the experience could better meet the original criteria.

ITERATION / SYNTHESIS
CONCEPT REFINEMENT
06 / OUTCOME

Measure the experience, not just the artifact.

The project evaluated the solution across four criteria. The reported results were strong: usability 9/10, feasibility 10/10, desirability 9/10, with an implementation cost of approximately $60 against a target below $100.

9/10Usability

How well the experience worked for the intended users.

10/10Feasibility

How realistically the concept could be implemented.

9/10Desirability

How well the concept resonated with users.

The biggest lesson: research is most useful when it changes what you decide to build.
07 / REFLECTION

What I took forward.

HTE reinforced a simple principle I bring into product work: the interface is only one part of the experience. Understanding the people, environment, constraints, and success criteria first makes the eventual design more grounded.

It also strengthened my interest in research that directly informs product decisions—especially when the users and context are different from the designer's own experience.
